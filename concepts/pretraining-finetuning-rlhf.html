Pretraining, Fine-tuning & RLHF

A freshly pretrained language model is not a chatbot — it's a very good autocomplete with no particular interest in being helpful, honest, or safe. Getting from one to the other takes two more deliberate training stages.

01 The plain-English version

Pretraining is where a model reads an enormous amount of text from the internet and books, learning to predict the next word — this is where nearly all of its raw knowledge and language ability comes from, but the result just continues text, it doesn't follow instructions or hold a conversation reliably.

Fine-tuning then trains the model further on a smaller, curated set of example conversations — showing it what a good response to an instruction actually looks like. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) refines it further by having human raters compare pairs of model responses, training the model to produce outputs people actually prefer.

02 The formal version

Pretraining optimizes a single objective — next-token prediction — over trillions of tokens, using pure self-supervision (the "label" for each position is just the next real token, requiring no human annotation at this stage). This is enormously expensive but produces a general-purpose base model.

Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) continues training on a much smaller dataset of high-quality instruction-response pairs, shifting the model's behavior toward following instructions. RLHF then trains a separate reward model to predict which of two responses humans would prefer, and uses reinforcement learning (commonly PPO, or newer methods like DPO) to adjust the language model's outputs to maximize that predicted reward — effectively teaching the model human preferences it can't get from raw text prediction alone.

03 The flow

Each stage narrows the model's behavior: pretraining builds broad capability, fine-tuning adds instruction-following, RLHF adds preference alignment.

04 Worked example

The same prompt at each stage

Prompt: "Explain photosynthesis." A base model might continue with something like more homework questions on the same topic, since that's a statistically plausible continuation of text that started that way. After fine-tuning, it directly answers the question with an explanation. After RLHF, it answers in a way human raters preferred during training — likely clearer, better organized, and appropriately hedged where the science is uncertain, because responses with those qualities were rated more highly.

05 Where this shows up

Real-world use

Essentially every consumer-facing chatbot — ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini — goes through some version of this full pipeline. It's also why base models (available through some APIs) feel noticeably different to use than their chat-tuned counterparts, and why companies keep iterating on the fine-tuning and RLHF stages long after a base model is trained, since this is where most of the day-to-day behavior tuning happens.

06 Common misconceptions

✕

RLHF teaches the model new facts or knowledge. Nearly all factual knowledge comes from pretraining; RLHF shapes style, helpfulness, and safety behavior on top of what the model already knows.

✕

Fine-tuning and RLHF are the same thing. Fine-tuning uses direct supervised examples; RLHF uses a learned reward signal and reinforcement learning — a fundamentally different training setup that can capture more nuanced preferences than a fixed set of examples can.

✓

Correct framing: pretraining builds the model's raw capability; everything after that is about directing that capability toward being useful and safe — which is why alignment work is a continuous, ongoing process, not a single step.
